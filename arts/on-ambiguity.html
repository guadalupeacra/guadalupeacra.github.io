On Ambiguity

Reflection

Some questions feel unsatisfying because they refuse to choose one interpretation. In economics, we call this identification. In ordinary life, there are fewer names for it.

The impulse is often to resolve ambiguity as quickly as possible. But uncertainty can also be information: a signal that the model is missing a variable, that the story is incomplete, or that two explanations can coexist.

I like the idea that not every unanswered question is a failed question. Sometimes the right response is not to close the model, but to make the model more honest.
